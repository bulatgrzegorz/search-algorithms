# Results snapshot (2026-11-10): LuceneIndex vs SortedArrayIndex, real-world scenarios

Apple M2 (8 cores, 16 GB), macOS 26.5.2, .NET 10.0.3, BenchmarkDotNet 0.15.8. Only `LuceneIndex` and `SortedArrayIndex` registered.
Both datasets: `isin` (GLEIF ISINs, 12 chars) and `names` (GLEIF legal entity names, avg 28 chars, max 476), see `data/download.sh`.
Short runs, so treat this as orientation, not precision: typeahead is `JOB=short` (3 iterations); latency windows are 10 s, so p99.9 rests on ~10 queries.

```sh
DATASET=isin|names ./execute_typeahead.sh
DATASET=isin|names ./execute_latency.sh Medium 1000 10 0|100|1000
DATASET=isin|names ./execute_footprint.sh 10000000
HEAP_LIMIT_MB=1000|2000 ./execute_footprint.sh 1000000
```

## Findings

- **Memory is Lucene's real advantage**: 5–6x less heap than SortedArrayIndex on the full files (517 MB vs 2.66 GB for 9.4M ISINs), with the index on disk via MMap.
- **Lucene collapses under writes on ISINs** at 1000 queries/s: already at 100 writes/s, p90 is 0.1–0.5 s and the worst queries take ~1 s, as queries arrive faster than they finish.
  The closed-loop `SearchBenchmarks` (523 μs at 1000 writes/s, 2026-11-08) hid this. On names it holds at 100 writes/s; not yet explained.
- **Typeahead StartsWith**: SortedArrayIndex ~1000x faster on ISINs (0.7 μs vs 704 μs per typed query). It returns a slice; Lucene copies every hit of a 1-char prefix for the caller to read 10.
- **Filter**: close. Lucene wins on names (305 vs 387 μs per typed query): an item with repeated chars has several entries in SortedArrayIndex's substring array, which then need de-duplicating.
- **Cold start**: Lucene's first query builds its `IdsByDoc` id cache, 7.9 s at 9.4M items; its build is 10x slower too (257 s vs 25 s).
- **SortedArrayIndex has tail spikes on names with writes**: p99.9 31–45 ms. Likely its merges allocating large arrays (unconfirmed).
